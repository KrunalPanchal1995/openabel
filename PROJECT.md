# Project form (Hello FOSS)

**Project name:** openabel

**Short description:** Learn how one side-view photo reveals the inside of a flame: line integrals, the Abel transform, inversion and noise, in 3 sessions.

**Full description:** openabel is a beginner-friendly learning repo about a clever trick used in combustion labs and plasma physics: if a glowing object is round about an axis, one photo from the side is enough to recover its 3D glow. In three short sessions you go from adding up light along a line of sight (a glowing ball), to the forward Abel transform of a radial profile with a design question about how many pixels you need, to the inverse by onion peeling and by a smoothed (regularised) method, tested against the exact pair Gaussian to Gaussian, with a measured look at noise amplification and the bias you pay for smoothing. A mini-project recovers the inside of a candle-like flame from a synthetic photo. Every session follows the same six steps (real-world problem, theory, visual explanation, interactive simulation, worked example, assessment) and has a lesson script, a Colab notebook, a live slider app and a checkpoint that prints PASS or FAIL. The repo is intentionally incomplete: a Target animation and a roadmap show where the community can take it next, such as finding the flame axis, choosing the smoothing automatically and rendering the result in 3D.

**Technologies (comma-separated):** Python, NumPy, Matplotlib, JavaScript, LaTeX
